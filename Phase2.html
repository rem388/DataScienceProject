Research Question:

How do patterns of leisure activities, including television viewing, social media use, reading, and exercise, differ across age groups, and how are these differences associated with cognitive and mental health assessment outcomes?

Research on prolonged passive media consumption (such as binge-watching or "doomscrolling") shows that these activities negatively affect cognitive function and mental-wellbeing. Alternatively, active leisure activities such as reading and exercise are frequently associated with positive cognitive and psychological outcomes.

Determining how leisure activities affect cognitive function and mental-wellbeing typically requires controlled experimental studies, which are difficult to conduct and beyond the scope of this project. Instead, we will investigate how leisure activity patterns vary across age groups and whether those patterns are associated with differences in cognitive and mental health assessments.

By combining datasets that measure leisure activities, demographic characteristics, and cognitive or mental health outcomes, we hope to identify meaningful relationships between lifestyle behaviors and well-being. In particular, we are interested in whether age groups characterized by greater participation in passive screen-based activities differ from groups that spend more time reading or exercising with respect to measures such as distractibility, forgetfulness, and overall mental health. While our analysis will not establish causation, it may reveal patterns that help motivate future research on how different forms of leisure activity relate to cognitive and psychological well-being.

Datasets and Descriptions:¶

2025 American Time Use Survey: Activity Summary File:
The U.S. Census Bureau annually conducts the American Time Use Survey (ATUS), funded by the U.S Bereau of Labor Statistics, which measures how American's divide their time among daily activities. These activities are organized into major categories such as personal care, eating and drinking, household activities, work, education, leisure and sports, and communication, with each category further divided into more detailed subcategories.

ATUS participants are randomly selected from a subset of households that have completed their eighth month of interviews for the U.S. Census Population Survey. Participants are then interviewed once to collect time-use data of their previous day. Demographic information such as age, sex, martial status, occupation, income, and education are also recorded for each participant. Survey respondants understand that no identifiable information will be shared and that their contributing time-use data can be used for public analysis.

ATUS provides several microdatasets regarding information on the respondant, activity, and activity summary. We will use ATUS's Activity Summary File (the raw data), which includes demographical and time-use data for each of the 6,145 participants. Detailed descriptions of dataset variables are included in the data cleaning section below.

Cognitive Assessment Questionnaire Dataset:
The Cognitive Assessment Questionnaire was designed to collect information about the frequency of everyday cognitive failures related to memory, perception, and attention. This survey was conducted by Vijayalaxmi Deshmukh, published by the Mendeley Data repository, and does not include any information about possible funding.

A total of 1,399 participants completed a 25-question online survey in which they reported how often they experienced specific cognitive lapses during the previous six months. Responses were recorded on a 5-point scale ranging from 0 (Never) to 4 (Very Often).

The questionnaire items are grouped into three dimensions of cognitive failure: Distractibility, Forgetfulness, and False Triggering. The dataset includes both the original survey responses and a processed version that classifies participants' levels of cognitive failure within each dimension as Low, Medium, or High. Detailed descriptions of dataset variables are included in the data cleaning section below.

2025 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System Survey Data:
The U.S. Center for Disease Control and Prevention funds and annually conducts the Behavior Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS) Survey, which interviews Americans on their health behaviors including if they exercise and how frequently they have bad mental health days. The purpose of the BRFSS is to help local governements monitor health disparities and address emerging public health situations.

The total sample for the 2025 BRFSS is 356,158. We are interested in respondant's age, sleep duration, and frequency of bad mental health days. Detailed descriptions of dataset variables and data processing methods are included in the data cleaning section below.

Health and Retirement Study (HRS) 2024:
The University of Michigan Health and Retirement Study (HRS) is a longitudinal panel study that surveys approximately 20,000 people in the United States, collecting information on participants’ health, cognition, employment, finances, family structure, and lifestyle. The HRS began in 1992 and has continued across multiple survey waves, with its most recent version conducted in 2024.

We are using the 2024 HRS data, which contain 14,398 respondent records. More specifically, the Leave-Behind Questionnaire (h24lb_r.csv) contains information about participants’ leisure activities, including reading, educational or training activities, television viewing, computer use, exercise, and walking. The Cognition file (h24d_r.csv) contains measures of cognitive performance, including immediate and delayed word-recall tasks, which we will use as measures of memory. The Physical Health file (h24c_r.csv) contains measures related to sleep quality and daily sedentary behavior, while the Demographics file (h24a_r.csv) provides participant characteristics, such as age, that may be used for comparisons with other datasets.

Cleaning BRFSS Dataset¶

BRFSS's 2025 raw data file includes 356,158 participants with over 200 variables, so we loaded it into jupyter lab and used BRFSS's Calculated Variables Document to determine 4 relevant variables.

Variables

  • SEQNO: participant's unique ID
  • _AGE80: participant's age
  • _TOTINDA: integer that track's participant's physical activity:
    - (1) participant exercised within the last 30 days
    - (2) participant has not exercised within the last 30 days
    - (9) participant doesn't know/refuses to answer/missing data
  • _MENT14D: integer that track's participant's bad mental health days within the past 30 days:
    - (1) participant had no bad mental health days
    - (2) participant had 1-13 bad mental health days
    - (3) participant had 14+ bad mental health days
    - (9) participant doesn't know/refuses to answer/missing data

Relevant Variables¶

Participant identifiers

  • HHID: household identification number
  • PN: respondent person number within the household When importing data we made both text strings because HRS specifically warns that identifiers such as HHID and PN may contain leading zeros and that users should preserve them when importing the data.

Leave-Behind Questionnaire (h24lb_r.csv)

  • TLB001E: frequency of attending an educational or training course
  • TLB001I: frequency of reading books, magazines, or newspapers
  • TLB001J: frequency of watching television
  • TLB001N: frequency of using a computer for email, Internet, or other tasks
  • TLB001S: frequency of playing sports or exercising
  • TLB001T: frequency of walking for 20 minutes or more

Cognition (h24d_r.csv)

  • TD174: number of words correctly recalled during the immediate word-recall task
  • TD184: number of words correctly recalled during the delayed word-recall task

Physical Health (h24c_r.csv)

  • TC083: frequency of having trouble falling asleep
  • TC084: frequency of waking during the night
  • TC085: frequency of waking too early and being unable to fall asleep again
  • TC086: frequency of feeling rested when waking in the morning
  • TC303: minutes reported daily sitting time
  • TC304: hours reported daily sitting time

Demographics (h24a_r.csv)

  • TA019: respondent's current age

Creating Total Sitting Time¶

The HRS records daily sitting time using separate variables for hours and minutes. These variables represent two components of the same reported sitting time. We keep the original variables in our dataset, but remove special survey response codes (negative time) before using them in calculations.

After cleaning, we combine the hour and minute components into a single variable representing total daily sitting time in minutes. This provides a more interpretable measure of sedentary behavior for our later analysis.
